Written out, one piece per word, last word first

Heard it differently?

Write how you would spell what you just heard. Whole line, one piece per word, last word first. These teach it far faster than the single-sound recordings do, because the rules that matter — glides, squashed vowels, dropped breaths — only show up at word length.

Spelled from recorded answers about what reversed speech actually sounds like: a reversed t arrives fricated, so it is written th or sth; a reversed k becomes kh; most pieces open on a breath. Odd as it looks, saying it that way reverses back cleaner than the tidy spelling would. Trust your ears where they disagree.

3 Say it backwards

Two ways to do this, and they answer different questions. Reading the script tests the spelling. Copying the sound tests you. Flat, even and slightly slow works either way.

Your take, reversed

Make the spelling yours

The written script is spelled the way a speech synthesiser reads it, which is not how you read it. Teach it your spelling — say a few words, hear them backwards, write down what you would actually read. The pieces are ordered by how much work they do, and the first fifty cover half of everything you will ever type.

Getting a clean take

  • Flat and slow beats expressive. Pitch and stress come back inside out, so they only muddy it.
  • Hold the vowels and put a small gap between pieces. Reversal eats anything you rush.
  • Two sounds fight you, and it is English, not the tool: a word starting with h needs a breath at the end of a piece, and a word ending in ng needs a piece that starts with that sound, which English never does.
